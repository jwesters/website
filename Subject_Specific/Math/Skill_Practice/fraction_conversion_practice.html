Fraction Conversion Practice

Convert mixed numbers and improper fractions.

Build the fraction. Count the pieces.

Students learn how a mixed number and an improper fraction can show the same amount. The tutorial uses shaded fraction bars and grouped pieces, then the practice test gives random questions with review at the end.

Example: 2 3/4 = 11/4
Two wholes make 8 fourths. Add 3 more fourths. That gives 11 fourths altogether.

Tutorial

Choose a skill and follow the pieces.

Mixed number → improper fraction

A mixed number has whole amounts and a fraction amount. For example, 2 3/4 means 2 whole bars and 3 fourth-size pieces.

To change it into an improper fraction, count how many same-size pieces are shaded in total.

  1. 1Look at the denominator. In 2 3/4, the denominator is 4, so each whole is cut into 4 equal pieces.
  2. 2Change the wholes into pieces: 2 × 4 = 8. Two wholes are the same as 8 fourths.
  3. 3Add the fraction pieces: 8 + 3 = 11. There are 11 fourths altogether.
  4. 4Keep the denominator because the pieces are still fourths: 2 3/4 = 11/4.

Build 2 3/4, then count fourths

2 × 4 + 3 = 11, so 2 3/4 = 11/4

The denominator stays 4 because every piece is still one fourth.

Improper fraction → mixed number

An improper fraction has enough pieces to make one or more wholes. For example, 11/4 means 11 fourth-size pieces.

To change it into a mixed number, group the pieces into full wholes.

  1. 1Use the denominator to know how many pieces make one whole. Fourths need 4 pieces per whole.
  2. 2Group 11 fourths into groups of 4. You can make 2 full wholes.
  3. 3There are 3 fourths left over.
  4. 4The answer is 2 3/4. The remainder becomes the numerator and the denominator stays 4.

Group 11 fourths into wholes

11 ÷ 4 = 2 remainder 3, so 11/4 = 2 3/4

The quotient is the whole number. The remainder is the fraction numerator.

Helpful checks

  1. 1Keep the denominator. If the pieces are fourths, the answer should still use fourths.
  2. 2Multiply before adding. For 3 2/5, do 3 × 5 + 2, not 3 + 2.
  3. 3Use the remainder correctly. For 17/6, 17 ÷ 6 = 2 remainder 5, so the answer is 2 5/6.
  4. 4Picture the pieces. If your answer says 17/6, ask yourself: can I group 17 sixths into wholes?

Quick visual check

Three full groups of fifths plus two more fifths is 17 fifths, not 5 fifths.

3 × 5 + 2 = 17, so 3 2/5 = 17/5
